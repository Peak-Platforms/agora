What Is an Agora?

In ancient Greek city-states, the agora (ἀγορά) was the open public square at the center of daily life — part marketplace, part town hall, part meeting ground. It was where merchants traded, citizens debated civic matters, and philosophers taught in the open air.

Under Roman rule, the great Greek agoras — including the one in Athens — remained the beating heart of public life even as Rome built its own forums in the same spirit. It was in that very agora that the apostle Paul stood and reasoned daily with everyone who passed through, Jew and Greek alike.

"Meanwhile he reasoned in the synagogue with the Jews and the devout persons, and in the marketplace daily with those who happened to be there."Acts 17:17

Agora Broadcasting Network carries that same idea into a web-native broadcast platform — a public square where faith and community still gather, still speak, and still are heard.
